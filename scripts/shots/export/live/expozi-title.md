# Title
Effect of Caffeine Intake on Sleep Quality in Adults

## Abstract
This study investigates the impact of caffeine intake on sleep quality in adults. Using a placebo-controlled, double-blind, randomized crossover design, 23 males with moderate caffeine intake were assessed. The results indicate no significant effect of 100 mg caffeine consumed 12 hours before bedtime on objective or subjective sleep quality. However, higher caffeine consumption was associated with decreased sleep time, suggesting a negative relationship between caffeine intake and sleep quality.

## Introduction
Caffeine is a widely consumed stimulant that can affect sleep quality and quantity. This study aims to explore the effects of caffeine intake on sleep quality in adults, specifically examining the impact of a typical dose of caffeine and a high dose consumed in different times of the day. The study design includes a placebo-controlled, double-blind, randomized crossover design to ensure the validity of the results.

## Key Findings
### Objective Sleep Quality
[1] No significant effect on objective sleep quality was observed with the 100 mg dose of caffeine consumed 12 hours before bedtime compared to the placebo. The lack of significant difference in objective sleep quality suggests that moderate caffeine intake does not significantly impair sleep quality.

### Subjective Sleep Quality
[1] No significant effect on subjective sleep quality was observed with the 100 mg dose of caffeine consumed 12 hours before bedtime compared to the placebo. The lack of significant difference in subjective sleep quality also suggests that moderate caffeine intake does not significantly impair subjective sleep quality.

### Total Caffeine Consumption and Sleep Quality
[2] Higher total caffeine consumption was associated with decreased time in bed, as an estimate of sleep time (r = -0.15, p = 0.04). This suggests that higher caffeine intake is associated with shorter sleep duration, indicating a negative relationship between caffeine consumption and sleep quality.

## Conclusion
The study found no significant effect of moderate caffeine intake (100 mg) consumed 12 hours before bedtime on objective or subjective sleep quality. However, higher total caffeine consumption was associated with decreased sleep time, indicating a negative relationship between caffeine intake and sleep quality. These findings suggest that while moderate caffeine intake does not significantly impair sleep quality, higher caffeine consumption may lead to shorter sleep duration.
