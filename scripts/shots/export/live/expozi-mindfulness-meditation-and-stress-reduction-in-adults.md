# Mindfulness Meditation and Stress Reduction in Adults
## Abstract
This report examines the efficacy of mindfulness meditation (MM) in reducing stress and its related symptoms in adults. Evidence from randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and clinical trials suggests that MM can be an effective stress-reducing method, particularly when compared to self-help physical activity (PA) and heart rate variability biofeedback (HRV-BF). Additionally, MM has shown potential in treating anxiety disorders, chronic low back pain, and social anxiety disorder, indicating its broad applicability in stress reduction.

## Introduction
Stress is a prevalent issue in contemporary western societies, leading to the need for effective stress-reducing methods. Mindfulness meditation (MM) has emerged as a popular and promising approach for stress reduction. This report synthesizes evidence from various RCTs and clinical trials to evaluate the efficacy of MM in reducing stress and related symptoms in adults.

## Key Findings
### Stress Reduction
[1] A randomized controlled trial compared self-help physical activity (PA), MM, and heart rate variability biofeedback (HRV-BF) in reducing stress and related symptoms. The study found that MM was as effective as PA in reducing stress and its symptoms. [2] A randomized clinical trial (TAME) compared MBSR to escitalopram, a commonly used first-line psychopharmacological treatment for anxiety disorders. The results indicated that MBSR was noninferior to escitalopram, suggesting that MBSR is a viable alternative to standard treatments for anxiety disorders. [3] A pilot study assessed the feasibility and combined effect of aerobic exercise (AE) and MM compared to MM alone and a control condition. Both AE + MM and MM were found to be feasible strategies for reducing stress, anxiety, and depression in high-stress college-based young adults.
